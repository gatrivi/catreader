Starter canon

Twelve is right: eight source books + four guardrails. There is no single Arthurian–Druidic–Irish–Orthodox lineage; these are distinct traditions that can meet inside the game.

Core eight
The Rule of St. Benedict — Timothy Fry translation
Read whole. Best foundation for daily rhythm, work, prayer, hospitality, discipline and community roles.
The Orthodox Way — Kallistos Ware
The theological grammar: incarnation, Trinity, prayer, theosis. Read before borrowing Orthodox imagery or the Jesus Prayer.
John Cassian: The Conferences — Boniface Ramsey translation
The East–West bridge and best source for “spiritual combat.” Start with Conferences 1–2, 5, 9–11 and 14: purpose, discernment, passions, prayer, love and knowledge.
The Ladder of Divine Ascent — John Climacus
Thirty stages from renunciation through struggle with passions to prayer and love. Almost suspiciously perfect for Cat Crusader—but treat it as a spiritual map, never an XP ladder.
Celtic Spirituality — Oliver Davies and Thomas O’Loughlin
Actual Irish and Welsh texts, not modern “druid vibes”: prayers, poetry, monastic rules, penitentials, pilgrimage and saints’ lives.
The Quest of the Holy Grail — Pauline Matarasso translation
The central Cat Crusader narrative: temptation, failure, chastity, confession, Eucharist, discernment and grace. Probably the single most directly useful book.
Robin Hood and Other Outlaw Tales — Knight and Ohlgren
Begin with Robin Hood and the Monk, Robin Hood and the Potter and A Gest of Robyn Hode. The medieval Robin is strikingly Marian: he hears Mass, serves Our Lady, receives strangers and rescues an indebted knight. The Gest itself may be your cleanest bridge from Rosario Cards.
What Were the Crusades? — Jonathan Riley-Smith, 4th edition
Short, essential definition of crusading: vow, pilgrimage, penance, indulgence and authorized warfare—not merely Christian-looking knights.
Four guardrails
King Arthur: The Making of the Legend — Nicholas Higham
Separates evidence from invented historical Arthur. Arthur is primarily a developing Brittonic/Welsh legend, not an identifiable proto-Orthodox Druid king.
Robin Hood — J. C. Holt, 3rd edition
Separates the early outlaw from Richard the Lionheart, Maid Marian, Friar Tuck and “steal from the rich,” much of which accumulated later.
Byzantium and the Crusades — Jonathan Harris, 3rd edition
Prevents the biggest conceptual error: Orthodox Byzantines and Latin crusaders were sometimes allies, frequently suspicious, and eventually enemies—not one knightly church.
Holy Warriors — Richard Kaeuper
The indispensable moral problem: how knights and clergy reconciled—or failed to reconcile—Christian discipleship with organized violence.
